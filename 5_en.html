Poem 5
奥山おくやまに 紅葉もみじ踏ふみ分わけ 鳴なく鹿しかの
声こえ聞きく時ときぞ 秋あきはかなしき
Author: Sarumaru Dayū (dates unknown)
Source: Kokin Wakashū, Autumn
English Verse Translation
Hear the stag's pathetic call Far up the mountain side, While tramping o'er the maple leaves Wind-scattered far and wide This sad, sad autumn tide.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
Deep in the mountains, treading through the scattered autumn leaves, I hear a deer cry. Listening to that voice, I feel the sadness of autumn all the more.
Commentary
According to the Kokin Wakashū's headnote, this poem was composed at a poetry contest held at the residence of Prince Koresada, a son of Emperor Kōkō. Autumn is the deer's rutting season, when stags call out in a distinctive high-pitched cry as they search for mates. This mournful cry seems to have been a seasonal sound that, for people of the time, evoked the deepening melancholy of autumn.
About the Poet
No record survives of Sarumaru Dayū's birth, death, or life story, and nothing concrete is known of his background or achievements — for this reason he is considered a legendary, semi-mythical poet.

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

Treading through the colored leaves, I realize how far behind
the world has fallen. When the deer's cry echoes from the mountain depths,
the loneliness I'd held inside comes spilling out.
