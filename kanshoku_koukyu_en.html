The Inner Palace (Kōkyū, 後宮)

The kōkyū was the section of the palace where the emperor's consorts and ladies-in-waiting lived and supported the emperor's daily life. It included the residential halls of the consorts and the administrative offices (the Twelve Offices of the Inner Palace, kōkyū-jūnishi) staffed by female officials.

Imperial Consorts

Title Description
Nyōgo (女御) One of the emperor's consorts; ranked just below the chūgū or kōgō (empress). Usually the daughter of a powerful noble. Often called by the name of her residential hall (e.g., Kokiden-no-nyōgo). A strong candidate to be elevated to empress.
Kōi (更衣) A consort ranked below the nyōgo. Generally from a family of lower standing, but could wield great influence if she enjoyed the emperor's deep affection.
Miyasudokoro (御息所) Originally an honorific for the mother who lived in the residential hall where imperial children were raised. Later, the term came to refer to any woman who had borne the emperor's child.

Ladies-in-Waiting and Female Officials (Nyōbō / Nyokan)

The daily lives of the consorts were directly supported by women known as nyōbō (ladies-in-waiting) and nyokan (female officials). Ladies-in-waiting managed everyday tasks and documentation close to their mistresses, and were ranked as jōrō (upper), chūrō (middle), and gerō (lower) according to their family background. Female officials had a variety of titles reflecting their specific duties — from presenting memorials to the emperor, to managing clothing, food, and medicine in the inner palace.

Title Description
Nyōbō (女房) Women who served close to a noble person in the palace or an aristocratic household, handling practical tasks, documentation, and daily assistance.
Meshi-udo (召人) A lady-in-waiting who received the favour of her master and sometimes entered into a concubine-like relationship.
Kami no Nyōbō (上の女房) Ladies-in-waiting who served directly at the emperor's side; mainly belonged to the Naishi no Tsukasa (Bureau of Handmaids).
Miya no Nyōbō (宮の女房) Women who served in the household organization of the empress or her guardian noble. They were ranked as jōrō (senior ladies, from families of 3rd rank or above) > chūrō (middle ladies, from families of 4th–5th rank) > gerō (junior ladies).
Nyōbō Sanyaku (女房三役)
Three Key Posts		 Senji (宣旨) · Mikushigedono-bettō (御匣殿別当) · Naishi no Kami (尚侍) — these three were considered the most prestigious ladies-in-waiting posts and were later collectively known as the "Three Key Posts" (sanyaku).
Senji (宣旨) A senior lady-in-waiting responsible for conveying the emperor's wishes within the ladies-in-waiting community; known as the senji nyōbō.
Mikushigedono (御匣殿) The mikushigedono was the place in the inner palace where female officials in charge of clothing and sewing worked. The woman who managed this office was called Mikushigedono-bettō and was sometimes referred to simply as Mikushigedono.
Naishi no Kami (尚侍) The head of the Naishi no Tsukasa (Bureau of Handmaids), serving as a close attendant and secretary to the emperor. In earlier times the position carried considerable political influence, but during the Heian period duties became increasingly focused on inner palace affairs.
Myōbu (命婦) A title given to female officials of 5th rank or above, or to the wives of male officials of 5th rank or above.
Nyokurodō (女蔵人) Lower-ranking ladies-in-waiting who served in the palace under the naishi and myōbu, attending to miscellaneous duties.
Tokusen (得選) Female officials of the imperial kitchen (Mizushi-dokoro) who assisted with food service and miscellaneous tasks. The name derives from the fact that they were selected from among the uneme.
Uneme (采女) Women chosen from the daughters of provincial lords to serve the emperor and empress in daily tasks such as waiting at table.
Nyoju (女嬬) Young women, mostly in their teens to around age thirty, who belonged to the Naishi no Tsukasa and were assigned miscellaneous duties.

The word bō (房) in nyōbō means "room": ladies-in-waiting were each given a personal chamber (a space partitioned by standing screens and hanging curtains) and lived within the palace while on duty.

Location of the Inner Palace

The inner palace where these women lived spread across the northern part of the dairi (imperial residence). There stood a cluster of residential halls known collectively as the Shichiden-Gosha (Seven Halls and Five Chambers), each of which served as a residence for the consorts and a place where serving women came and went. The "Kiritsubo" and "Fujitsubo" that appear in The Tale of Genji were among these halls.

The Twelve Offices of the Inner Palace (Kōkyū-jūnishi)

The vast inner palace was run by the kōkyū-jūnishi (Twelve Offices of the Inner Palace). Specialist offices covered every aspect of palace life — from the consorts' food, medicine, and clothing to the management of gate keys and cleaning.

Name Function
Naishi no Tsukasa (内侍司) Conveyed reports and petitions to the throne, relayed imperial commands, and oversaw inner palace ceremonial protocol.
Kura no Tsukasa (蔵司) Managed important items including the imperial seal (shinji), gate passes (kankei), and the clothing of the emperor and empress.
Fumi no Tsukasa (書司) Managed books and writing supplies such as paper, ink, and brushes.
Kusuri no Tsukasa (薬司) Managed medicines and oversaw the administration of medicine to the emperor, including poison-tasting.
Tsuwamono no Tsukasa (兵司) Managed ceremonial weapons and armour used in rites and ceremonies (not combat weapons, as the inner palace was a living space).
Mikado no Tsukasa (闈司) Managed the keys to the inner palace gates.
Tonomori no Tsukasa (殿司) Managed palanquins and their canopies, lighting, charcoal, and bathing supplies for palace life.
Kanimori no Tsukasa (掃司) Maintained palace facilities, set up furnishings, and managed cleaning.
Moitori no Tsukasa (水司) Managed the supply of drinking water and light refreshments such as rice porridge.
Kashiwade no Tsukasa (膳司) Managed the preparation, service, and poison-tasting of the emperor's meals.
Miki no Tsukasa (酒司) Managed the brewing of sake and the care of sake vessels.
Nui no Tsukasa (縫司) Managed the sewing of garments and the making of braided cords.

The Naishi no Tsukasa (Bureau of Handmaids)

Among the Twelve Offices of the Inner Palace, it was the Naishi no Tsukasa that grew particularly important during the Heian period. While the other eleven offices gradually became hollow institutions, the Naishi no Tsukasa alone maintained its role of conveying memorials and messages to the emperor, and became the de facto centre of the female official organisation within the inner palace.

The Naishi no Tsukasa was one of the Twelve Offices established by the legal codes (ryō), serving in close attendance on the emperor and handling memorials and communications. Under the ritsuryō system, twelve offices were placed in the inner palace, but from the mid-Heian period onward the rise of the Kurōdo-dokoro (Imperial Secretariat) caused the practical work of the other eleven offices to gradually decline until they became merely nominal. As a result, the Naishi no Tsukasa became the effective centre of the female official organisation in the inner palace.
