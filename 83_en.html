Poem 83
世よの中なかよ 道みちこそなけれ 思おもひい入いる
山やまの奥おくにも 鹿しかぞ鳴なくなる
Author: Fujiwara no Toshinari (1114–1204)
Source: Senzai Wakashū, Miscellaneous
English Verse Translation
From pain and sorrow all around There's no escape, I fear; To mountain wilds should I retreat, There also I should hear The cry of hunted deer.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
There is no escape from this world. Even deep in the mountains, the deer cry out.
Commentary
Toshinari wrote this around age 27, as the Heian period was nearing its end. Many people his age were becoming monks, and he may have gone into the mountains thinking of doing the same. But hearing a stag's sad cry deep in the mountains, he seems to have realized there was no escape from life's troubles.
About the Poet
Fujiwara no Toshinari was the father of Fujiwara no Sadaie, compiler of the Hyakunin Isshu, and compiled the Senzai Wakashū. As a judge at many contests, he often praised "yūgen" (deep, mysterious beauty) and "en" (elegance). His sense of beauty later shaped the tea ceremony and Noh as well.

Appendix
A Poem About Mono no Aware
This poem was written at a poetry gathering on the theme of "love." Toshinari praised The Tale of Genji as a work that embodies mono no aware (a deep feeling for the sadness and beauty of things), and left the words "A poet who has not read Genji is a regrettable thing." The poem says that through love, not only through its joys but also its sorrows and pain, people's hearts grow rich, and they come to understand mono no aware, the fleeting, painful beauty of life.

In Japanese

恋こいせずは 人ひとも心こころも なからまし
もののあはれも これよりぞ知しる Chōshū Eisō, Fujiwara no Toshinari

In English

Without love, people would never grow a heart.
It is through love that we come to understand mono no aware.

Chōshū Eisō is Toshinari's personal poetry collection.

A Love Poem
Toshinari sent this poem to a woman he was really in love with. We know this from its headnote: "Sent to a woman on a rainy day." The "haze" in the poem is thought to stand for his clouded feelings of love, and the "spring rain" for the tears he shed, sad that he could not meet her.

In Japanese

思おもひあまり そなたの空そらを ながむれば
霞かすみを分わけて 春雨はるさめぞ降ふる Shin Kokin Wakashū, Fujiwara no Toshinari

In English

Longing for you too much, I gaze at the sky where you live,
and spring rain falls, parting the haze.
How to Read the Name 俊成
The name 俊成 is read both "Toshinari" and "Shunzei."
Reading a name in the Chinese-style way, like "Shunzei," is called yūsoku-yomi.
