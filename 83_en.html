世よの中なかよ

やまのおく
にもしかそ
なくなる

よのなかよ

Poem 83
世よの中なかよ 道みちこそなけれ 思おもひい入いる
山やまの奥おくにも 鹿しかぞ鳴なくなる
Author: Fujiwara no Shunzei (1114–1204)
Source: Senzai Wakashū, Miscellaneous
English Verse Translation
From pain and sorrow all around There's no escape, I fear; To mountain wilds should I retreat, There also I should hear The cry of hunted deer.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
There is no escape from this world. Even deep in the mountains, the deer cry out.
Commentary
Shunzei wrote this around age 27, as the Heian period was nearing its end. Many people his age were becoming monks, and he may have gone into the mountains thinking of doing the same. But hearing a stag's sad cry deep in the mountains, he seems to have realized there was no escape from life's troubles.
About the Poet
Fujiwara no Shunzei was the father of Fujiwara no Teika, compiler of the Hyakunin Isshu, and compiled the Senzai Wakashū. As a judge at many contests, he often praised "yūgen" (deep, mysterious beauty) and "en" (elegance). His sense of beauty later shaped the tea ceremony and Noh as well.
