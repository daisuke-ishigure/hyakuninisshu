Poem 69
嵐あらし吹ふく 三室みむろの山やまの 紅葉もみじ葉ばは
竜田たつたの川かわの 錦にしきなりけり
Author: Priest Nōin (988–1050 or 1058)
Source: Go Shūi Wakashū, Autumn
English Verse Translation
The storms, which round Mount Mimuro Are wont to howl and scream, Have thickly scattered maple leaves Upon Tatsuta's stream; Like red brocade they seem.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
The storm blows through Mimuro Mountain, and the scattered maple leaves become a brocade on the Tatsuta River.
Commentary
Written for a poetry contest at Emperor Go-Reizei's court on the theme "autumn leaves."
About the Poet
Priest Nōin studied literature at the court school but became a monk at 26. He loved utamakura, places famous in poetry, and traveled the country writing poems. One story says he tanned himself in the sun to pretend a poem written in Kyoto was written on a journey. Another says his prayer poem brought rain for three days in a drought.
