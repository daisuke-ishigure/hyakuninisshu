Poem 82
思おもひいわび さても命いのちは あるものを
憂うきにたへえぬは 涙なみだなりけり
Author: Priest Dōin (1090–death year unknown)
Source: Senzai Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
How sad and gloomy is the world, This world of sin and woe! Ah! while I drift along Life's stream, Tossed helpless to and fro, My tears will ever flow.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
I am exhausted with sorrow, yet somehow I am still alive. What cannot endure is not my life — but my tears.
Commentary
This poem expresses the pain of heartbreak, grieving over a lover's coldness. Even as an overwhelming sorrow washes over him, the poem reveals the poet's quiet resolve to keep on living regardless.
About the Poet
Priest Dōin, born Fujiwara no Atsuyori, served in the court post of Assistant Master of the Imperial Stables, overseeing the care of horses, and rose to the rank of Junior Fifth Rank, Upper. In hopes of composing ever-better poetry, he is said to have walked on foot from Kyoto to Sumiyoshi Taisha Shrine in Osaka every month between the ages of seventy and eighty. Even after taking Buddhist vows past eighty, he continued attending poetry gatherings well into his nineties, listening eagerly to critiques — a lifelong devotion to the art of poetry.

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

However much I try to hold back my tears,
they quietly fall down my cheeks.

And still, meaning to live through today,
I carry this sorrow close to my heart.
