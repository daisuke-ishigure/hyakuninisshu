Poem 31
朝あさぼらけ 有明ありあけの月つきと 見みるまでに
吉野よしのの里さとに 降ふれる白雪しらゆき
Author: Sakanoue no Korenori (dates unknown–930)
Source: Kokin Wakashū, Winter
English Verse Translation
Surely the morning moon, I thought, Has bathed the hill in light But, no; I see it is the snow That, falling in the night, Has made Yoshino white.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
In the early morning light, the village of Yoshino is blanketed in white — it looks just like the pale glow of the dawn moon.
Commentary
"Asaborake" means the moment just before dawn, used mostly in autumn and winter. Yoshino (Nara Prefecture) is now famous for cherry blossoms, but then it was known for snow. Yoshino, where Emperor Tenmu and Empress Jitō once withdrew, was a place Heian nobles dreamed of.
About the Poet
Sakanoue no Korenori was a descendant of Sakanoue no Tamuramaro, the general who conquered the Emishi. He once served as an official in Yamato Province (Nara), so he probably knew Yoshino well. Besides poetry, he was famous as a master of kemari (court football).

Sakanoue no Korenori was a descendant of Sakanoue no Tamuramaro, who, as Seii Taishōgun (shogun), made the Emishi, the people of the northeast who resisted the court, surrender.

Korenori was very good at kemari (court football, keeping a ball in the air by kicking it). It is said that in 905, at a game of kemari before Emperor Daigo, he kicked the ball 206 times without once letting it fall, and the emperor gave him silk as a reward.

Appendix
The Ariake Moon and the Phases of the Moon

The ariake no tsuki is the moon that is still in the sky after dawn. From about the 15th to the 29th day of the lunar month, the moon can be seen in the early-morning sky.

In the Heian period, only privileged people could have calendars. So for ordinary people, the changing shape of the moon probably served as a kind of natural calendar.

Li Bai's Chinese Poem "Quiet Night Thoughts"
Sakanoue no Korenori's Hyakunin Isshu poem is thought to draw on the scene of "Quiet Night Thoughts" (Seiyashi), a famous Chinese poem by Li Bai. In "Quiet Night Thoughts," moonlight shining on the ground is seen as "frost on the ground," and the poet longs for his faraway home. Korenori, in turn, saw the white snow piled up in the village of Yoshino as "the light of the dawn moon left in the sky," and captured the quiet beauty of a winter daybreak. Here we see a tribute in verse across time and place.

In Chinese

牀前しょうぜん看月光
疑是地上霜
挙頭望山月
低頭思故郷
"Quiet Night Thoughts," Li Bai

In English

Before my bed, I see the moonlight.
I wonder if it is frost on the ground.
I raise my head and gaze at the mountain moon,
I lower my head and think of home.

Sakanoue no Korenori, a Provincial Official
In the capital, Sakanoue no Korenori held posts such as Senior Secretary (Dainaiki), and he also served in a series of provincial posts (as a kokushi, provincial official), such as Acting Junior Secretary of Yamato, Senior Secretary of Yamato, and Assistant Governor of Kaga. According to the headnote in the Kokin Wakashū, he wrote his Hyakunin Isshu poem while serving as Acting Junior Secretary of Yamato. For the central government offices, see this page.
