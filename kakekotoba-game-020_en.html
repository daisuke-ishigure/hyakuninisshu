Spin & Discover Kakekotoba!
Poem 20: Prince Motoyoshi

Try to find the kakekotoba hidden in the poem and tap it.
The word you tap will spin right where it is, revealing its two meanings in turn.

わびぬれば
いまはたおなじ
難波なる
みをつくしても
逢はむとぞ思ふ

Commentary

The word "mi o tsukushi" is a kakekotoba.
A kakekotoba layers two meanings onto a single word that sounds the same either way.

Here, "mi o tsukushi" carries both "澪標みおつくし" (channel markers) and "身みを尽つくし" (giving everything of myself).

I have already suffered so much heartbreak that nothing else matters now. Like the channel markers standing in the sea at Naniwa, I would give everything of myself just to see you once more. It is a passionate poem of love, layering the fierce resolve of "giving everything of myself" onto a scene tied to a place name.

Packing two meanings into just thirty-one syllables is exactly what makes kakekotoba so appealing.

Vocabulary

わびぬれば

Because I have already suffered so much heartbreak.

いまはたおなじ

Now it no longer makes a difference either way.

難波なにわなる

That which is in Naniwa. "Naru" expresses existence.

澪標みおつくし

Wooden markers set up in the sea at Naniwa to show ships the safe channel.

身みを尽つくし

Giving everything of myself, staking all I have for you.

逢はむとぞ思ふ

I intend to meet you. "Mu" expresses intention, "zo" adds emphasis.
