Poem 41
恋こいすてふちょう 我わが名なはまだき 立たちにけり
人ひとしれずこそ 思おもひいそめしか
Author: Mibu no Tadami (dates unknown)
Source: Shūi Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
Our courtship, that we tried to hide, Misleading is to none; And yet how could the neighbours guess, That I had yet begun To fancy any one?
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
They say my name as a lover is already known far and wide, even before the affair began. I had kept it so quietly in my heart.
Commentary
Written for the Tentoku palace poetry contest (960) on the theme "love not yet met," it was evenly matched with Taira no Kanemori's poem, but lost because Emperor Murakami was heard reciting Kanemori's. A tale in the Shasekishū says Tadami was so crushed that he stopped eating, fell ill, and died.
About the Poet
Mibu no Tadami was the son of Mibu no Tadamine and a low-ranking official under Emperor Murakami. He inherited his father's gift and wrote fine poems from childhood. Called to the palace, he worked in the imperial kitchen office and later became an official of Settsu Province.
