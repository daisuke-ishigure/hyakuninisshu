Poem 19
難波潟なにわがた みじかき葦あしの ふしのまも
逢あはわでこの世よを 過すぐしてよとや
Author: Ise (dates unknown)
Source: Shin Kokin Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
Short as the joints of bamboo reeds That grow beside the sea On pebble beach at Naniwa, I hope the time may be, When thou 'rt away from me.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
In this short life, even as brief as a joint of reed at Naniwa Bay, must I pass through this world without ever meeting you?
Commentary
The Ise Shū's headnote reads, "In autumn, when someone said something unpleasant." This poem is said to express Ise's complicated feelings after being abandoned by her former lover, Fujiwara no Nakahira.
About the Poet
Ise was one of the leading poets of the Kokin Wakashū era, and her life was marked by many romances. After parting from Fujiwara no Nakahira, she bore a son to Emperor Uda and came to be known as "Ise no Miyasudokoro." She later had a daughter, Nakatsukasa, with Emperor Uda's son Prince Atsuyoshi — Nakatsukasa would go on to become a celebrated poet in her own right.

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

Even just a moment —
all I want is to see you.

If even that wish won't be granted,
what am I supposed to live for?
