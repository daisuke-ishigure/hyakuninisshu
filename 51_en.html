Poem 51
かくとだに えやはいぶきの さしも草ぐさ
さしも知しらじな もゆる思おもひいを
Author: Fujiwara no Sanekata Ason (dates unknown–998)
Source: Go Shūi Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
Though love, like blisters made from leaves Grown on Mount Ibuki, Torments me more than I can say, My lady shall not see, How she is paining me.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
I cannot even tell you how I feel — like the smoldering fire of ibuki wormwood, my hidden love quietly burns.
Commentary
The headnote in the Go Shūi Wakashū reads, "Sent upon returning home from a woman's residence on a day when snow was falling." One theory holds that this poem was sent to Sei Shōnagon.
About the Poet
Fujiwara no Sanekata was a great-grandson of Teishin-kō (Poem 26), born into an illustrious family descended from the regental line, and much was expected of his future. However, after a quarrel with Fujiwara no Yukinari at court that led him to fling Yukinari's ceremonial hat to the ground, he incurred the anger of Emperor Ichijō. As a result, he was ordered to "go see the famous poetic places for yourself" and was demoted to a post in Mutsu Province. This anecdote is recorded in the Kojidan, and has come down to us as an episode symbolizing his turbulent life.

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

I can't even bring myself
to say plainly that I love you.

This unspoken love of mine
keeps quietly burning in my chest, even now.
