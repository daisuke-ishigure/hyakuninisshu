Spin & Discover Kakekotoba!
Poem 88: Attendant to Empress Kōka

Try to find the kakekotoba hidden in the poem and tap it.
The word you tap will spin right where it is, revealing its two meanings in turn.

難波江の
葦のかりねの
ひとよゆゑ
みをつくしてや
恋ひわたるべき

Commentary

This poem has three kakekotoba hidden within it.
A kakekotoba layers two meanings onto a single word that sounds the same either way.

The first is "karine." It carries both "刈かり根ね" (the stubble left after reeds are cut) and "仮寝かりね" (a brief, temporary sleep).

The second is "hitoyo." It carries both "一節ひとよ" (the short space between two joints of a reed stalk) and "一夜ひとよ" (a single night).

The third is "mi o tsukushi." It carries both "澪標みおつくし" (channel markers) and "身みを尽つくし" (giving everything of myself).

Must I really go on giving everything of myself, burning with longing for you forever, all because of a single night's brief encounter — as fleeting as the space between one joint of a reed stalk growing in the inlets of Naniwa?

Stringing together words associated with reeds, the poem expresses an aching love that has only deepened since one all-too-short meeting.

Packing so many meanings into just thirty-one syllables is exactly what makes kakekotoba so appealing.

Vocabulary

難波江なにわえ

An inlet in Naniwa (present-day Osaka), where reeds grew thickly.

葦あし

A tall plant that grows near water.

刈かり根ね

The stubble left in the ground after reeds are cut down.

仮寝かりね

A lodging away from home, on a journey.

一節ひとよ

The space between two joints of a reed stalk.
In waka poetry, sometimes used as a metaphor for something brief.

ゆゑ

Because of ~, on account of ~.

澪標みおつくし

A marker post set up in shallow water prone to running aground, showing where the water is deep enough for ships to pass safely.

こひわたるべき

Must I go on and on longing for you?
