Poem 58
ありま山やま 猪名いなの笹原ささはら 風かぜ吹ふけば
いでそよ人ひとを 忘わすれやはする
Author: Daini no Sanmi (c. 999–c. 1082)
Source: Go Shūi Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
As fickle as the mountain gusts That on the moor I've met, 'Twere best to think no more of thee, And let thee go. But yet I never can forget.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
When the wind blows through the bamboo groves of Arima and Ina — of course I have not forgotten you.
Commentary
A man who had not written for a long time sent her a letter asking whether her feelings had changed. This poem was her reply.
About the Poet
Daini no Sanmi (Katako) was Murasaki Shikibu's daughter and, like her mother, served Empress Akiko. She later became nurse to the future Emperor Go-Reizei, and after he took the throne she rose to Junior Third Rank (sanmi). "Daini" comes from the post of her second husband, Takashina no Nariakira, at Dazaifu.

Like her mother, Murasaki Shikibu, Daini no Sanmi served Akiko, the empress of Emperor Ichijō.

She is known to have had relationships with Fujiwara no Yorimune, a son of Fujiwara no Michinaga; Fujiwara no Sadayori, a son of Fujiwara no Kintō; Minamoto no Tomotō; and others. Later she married Fujiwara no Kanetaka, a son of Michinaga's elder brother Fujiwara no Michikane, and had a daughter.

This birth became a turning point: when Akiko's grandson, Prince Chikahito (later Emperor Go-Reizei), was born, she was chosen as his nurse (menoto). When Emperor Go-Reizei took the throne, Daini no Sanmi was made a senior lady of the Palace Attendants' Office (naishi no suke) and was later given Junior Third Rank.

Daini no Sanmi later married Takashina no Nariakira and had a son, Takashina no Tameie, and a daughter. In 1054, her husband Nariakira was made Senior Assistant Governor-General of Dazaifu (Dazai no Daini). The name "Daini no Sanmi" combines her husband's post, "Daini," and her own rank, "Sanmi" (Third Rank).

In her later years she outlived her husband; records such as her taking part in poetry contests suggest that she lived to about 80.

Daini no Sanmi's Poem to Fujiwara no Yorimune
The headnote in the Go Shūi Wakashū says it was "sent to the Horikawa Minister of the Right."
Daini no Sanmi sent this poem to Fujiwara no Yorimune, a son of Fujiwara no Michinaga.

In Japanese

恋こいしさの 憂うきにまぎるる ものならば
またふたたびと 君きみを見みましや koishisano ukinimagiruru mononaraba
matafutatabito kimiomimashiya Go Shūi Wakashū, Daini no Sanmi

In English

If my longing for you could be forgotten among the troubles of daily life, would I want to see you again? (No, it cannot be forgotten, so I want to see you.)
Poems Exchanged with Fujiwara no Sadayori
The next poems were exchanged between Fujiwara no Sadayori and Daini no Sanmi.
Fujiwara no Sadayori's Poem
Because he could hardly ever meet Daini no Sanmi, Sadayori wrote a poem saying that he looks at plum blossoms instead, thinking of them as her.
He sent the poem to her with a branch of plum blossoms.

In Japanese

見みぬ人ひとに よそへて見みつる 梅うめの花はな
散ちりなむ後あとの なぐさめぞなき minuhitoni yosohetemitsuru umenohana
chirinamuatono nagusamezonaki Shin Kokin Wakashū, Fujiwara no Sadayori

In English

I have been looking at these plum blossoms, thinking of you, whom I cannot meet. Once they fall, nothing will be left to comfort me.
Daini no Sanmi's Poem
Daini no Sanmi answered by comparing Sadayori to the plum branch.
She teases the fickle Sadayori, saying the branch carries the scent of another woman.

In Japanese

春はるごとに 心こころをしむる 花はなの枝えに
たがなほざりの 袖そでかふれつる harugotoni kokorooshimuru hananoeni
taganahozarino sodekafuretsuru Shin Kokin Wakashū, Daini no Sanmi

In English

Every spring this flowering branch has filled my heart. Whose careless sleeve has brushed against it and left its scent?
A Poem from the Man'yōshū
Poems about Inano and Mount Arima go back to the time of the Man'yōshū.

In Japanese

しなが鳥どり 猪名野いなのを来くれば 有馬山ありまやま
夕霧ゆうぎり立たちぬ 宿やどりはなくて shinagadori inanookureba arimayama
yuugiritachinu yadorihanakute Man'yōshū, Anonymous

In English

As I came across Inano, where the shinagadori birds fly,
the evening fog rose over Mount Arima,
and there is no place to stay.
Rozan-ji Temple (Stones Carved with the Poems of Daini no Sanmi and Murasaki Shikibu)

Rozan-ji in Kamigyō Ward, Kyoto, is known as a place connected with Daini no Sanmi. The mansion of her great-great-grandfather (four generations back), Fujiwara no Kanesuke, once stood in this area, and like her mother, Murasaki Shikibu, she was born and raised here.

The grounds have stones carved with the poems of Daini no Sanmi and Murasaki Shikibu, as well as a beautiful dry landscape garden of white sand and moss, the "Genji Garden." In summer, when they are at their best, lovely purple bellflowers (kikyō), which match the image of Murasaki Shikibu, color the garden.
