Poem 15
君きみがため 春はるの野のに出いでて 若菜わかなつむ
わが衣手ころもでに 雪ゆきは降ふりつつ
Author: Emperor Kōkō (830–887)
Source: Kokin Wakashū, Spring
English Verse Translation
Mother, for thy sake I have been Where the wakana grow, To bring thee back some fresh green leaves And see—my koromo Is sprinkled with the snow!
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
Going out into the spring fields to pick young greens for you — the snow falls on my sleeves.
Commentary
The Kokin Wakashū says the future Emperor Kōkō, while still a prince, sent this poem with a gift of young spring greens.
About the Poet
After Yōzei was removed by Fujiwara no Mototsune, Kōkō became emperor at 55 on Mototsune's recommendation. He died after only three years, and Mototsune held the real power. A history of the time calls him humble, gentle, kind, and generous, and that gentle nature shows in his poem.

Emperor Kōkō became emperor at the age of 55, after Retired Emperor Yōzei gave up the throne. Kōkō expected that when he himself stepped down, Prince Sadayasu, Emperor Yōzei's younger brother, would become the next emperor, so he had his own children leave the imperial family and become subjects.

However, the Kampaku (chief adviser to the emperor), Fujiwara no Mototsune, put Kōkō's seventh son, Prince Sadami, on the throne as Emperor Uda.

Appendix
A Poem Based on Kōkō's, in The Tales of Yamato
This poem appears in The Tales of Yamato (Yamato Monogatari) and is thought to be based on Emperor Kōkō's Hyakunin Isshu poem. In the story, Lieutenant Yoshimine (later High Priest Henjō) stops at a run-down house to get out of the rain and meets a beautiful woman there. The next day, she picks greens from the garden and cooks them for him. The poem shows her touching feelings: though she is poor, she does all she can to welcome her guest.

In English

These are young greens I went out to the spring fields to pick for you, getting the hem of my robe wet.

A Poem Sad That Snow Keeps Him
from Picking Young Greens
In this poem, Yamabe no Akahito had been looking forward to picking young greens, and he is disappointed that the snow keeps falling.

In Japanese

明日あすよりは 春菜はるな摘つまむと 標しめし野のに
昨日きのうも今日きょうも 雪ゆきは降ふりつつ asuyoriha harunatsumamuto shimeshinoni
kinoumokyoumo yukihafuritsutsu Man'yōshū, Yamabe no Akahito

In English

I marked a field so I could pick spring greens from tomorrow on, but yesterday and today, the snow keeps falling.

Utagawa Kuniyoshi's Hyakunin Isshu no Uchi

This ukiyo-e is by Utagawa Kuniyoshi, an artist of the Edo period.
It shows a scene that matches the poem in the Hyakunin Isshu.

According to the headnote in the Kokin Wakashū, Emperor Kōkō wrote his Hyakunin Isshu poem while he was still Prince Tokiyasu.

Even as a prince, he was a member of the imperial family, so he may really have gone out with a long line of attendants, as in this print.
