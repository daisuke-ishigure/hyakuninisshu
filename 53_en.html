Poem 53
嘆なげきつつ ひとり寝ぬる夜よの 明あくる間まは
いかに久ひさしき ものとかは知しる
Author: Mother of the Right Captain Michitsuna (c. 936–995)
Source: Shūi Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
All through the long and dreary night I lie awake and moan; How desolate my chamber feels, How weary I have grown Of being left alone!
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
Lying alone, waiting for the long night to end — how endless it feels. Does the person who left me even know?
Commentary
The poet married Fujiwara no Kaneie, who already had a chief wife. After she bore their first child, he came home less and began seeing another woman. One night she refused to let him in, and the next day sent him this poem with a single faded chrysanthemum.
About the Poet
The Mother of the Right Captain Michitsuna was a secondary wife of Fujiwara no Kaneie, who later became chief adviser to the emperor. Her Kagerō Diary tells of her pain over his affairs, but also shows a proud woman who stood up to a powerful man. She is counted among Japan's three great beauties.

Michitsuna's mother was one of the wives of Fujiwara no Kaneie. A wife who was not the principal wife had a weak position, and she often spent her days simply waiting, never knowing when her husband would visit.

Kaneie's principal wife was a woman named Tokihime. Their children included Fujiwara no Michitaka, who founded the Naka-no-Kanpaku family, and Fujiwara no Michinaga, who led regency politics (sekkan seiji) at its height.

Her niece was Sugawara no Takasue's Daughter, the author of the Sarashina Diary. Takasue's Daughter was a descendant of Sugawara no Michizane.

Appendix
Fujiwara no Yasuko
Women in the Heian period did not make their names public, so even Michitsuna's mother, who left a great diary, is not known by her real name. In the 2024 NHK historical drama Hikaru Kimi e (Dear Radiance), she appears under the name "Fujiwara no Yasuko." The makers of the drama probably made up this name themselves, taking one character from the name of her father, Fujiwara no Tomoyasu.
Fujiwara no Kaneie
A court noble of the mid-Heian period. He came from the regents' family (sekkanke), and as the grandfather of Emperor Ichijō on his mother's side, he himself became Regent and Kampaku (chief adviser). The Mother of the Right Captain Michitsuna was a secondary wife of Kaneie. Fujiwara no Michitaka, the husband of the Mother of the Honorary Grand Minister, was the eldest son of Kaneie and his principal wife, Tokihime.

Poems Exchanged on the Uji River

When Michitsuna's mother went on a pilgrimage to Hase, Kaneie came to Uji to meet her, and the two exchanged the following poems across the Uji River, which was covered in fog. "Uji" is a pun (kakekotoba) on the place name Uji and ushi (unkind).

Ajiro are stakes set up to catch hio (young sweetfish), a winter sight on the Uji River. For details, see this page.
