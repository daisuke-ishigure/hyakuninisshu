Spin & Discover Kakekotoba!
Poem 27: Middle Counselor Kanesuke

Try to find the kakekotoba hidden in the poem and tap it.
The word you tap will spin right where it is, revealing its two meanings in turn.

みかの原
わきて流るる
いづみ川
いつ見きとてか
恋しかるらむ

Commentary

The word "izumi" is a kakekotoba.
A kakekotoba layers two meanings onto a single word that sounds the same either way.

Here, "izumi" carries both "泉いずみ" (izumi, a spring) and "いつ見いつみ" (itsu mi, "when did I see [you]").

The Izumi River rises and flows, cutting across the Mika Plain. From the sound of the river's name comes the question "itsu miki tote ka" ("just when did I ever see you?") — expressing the poet's bewildered love: why do I long for you so deeply, when we have not even truly gotten to know each other?

Packing two meanings into just thirty-one syllables is exactly what makes kakekotoba so appealing.

Vocabulary

みかの原はら

The name of a plain on the north bank of the Kizu River, in southern Kyoto Prefecture.

湧わきて

Welling up.

分わきて

Cutting across, dividing. Both meanings overlap in the word "wakite."

とてか

Saying that — could it be ~?

恋しかるらむ

Must be why I long for you so.
"Ramu" expresses a guess at the present cause or reason.
