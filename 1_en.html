Poem 1
秋あきの田たの かりほの庵いおの 苫とまをあらみ
我わが衣手ころもでは 露つゆにぬれつつ
Author: Emperor Tenji (626–671)
Source: Gosen Wakashū, Autumn
English Verse Translation
Out in the fields this autumn day. They're busy reaping grain; I sought for shelter 'neath this roof, But fear I sought in vain,— My sleeve is wet with rain.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
By the rice fields, in a makeshift hut with a loosely woven roof, my sleeves are wet with the night dew.
Commentary
At harvest time, farmers would build makeshift huts near their rice fields and keep watch through the night to protect the crop from animals. It's hard to picture an emperor actually spending the night in such a hut with dew-soaked sleeves. For this reason, it is thought that an anonymous poem from the Man'yōshū was later passed down as a poem by Emperor Tenji — one that reflects his compassion for the common people.
About the Poet
Emperor Tenji drove the Taika Reforms, shifting power away from clan-based rule toward a government centered on the emperor. By laying the groundwork for a centralized state, he helped build the foundations of the Heian era and was deeply revered by the people of that time. He was also a direct ancestor of Emperor Kanmu, who founded the capital of Heian-kyō.

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

By the rice fields,
I keep watch in a rough little hut.

Even this cold night dew
is more beautiful than the shining court.
