Poem 32
山川やまがわに 風かぜのかけたる しがらみは
流ながれもあへえぬ 紅葉もみじなりけり
Author: Harumichi no Tsuraki (dates unknown–920)
Source: Kokin Wakashū, Autumn
English Verse Translation
The stormy winds of yesterday The maple branches shook; And see! a mass of crimson leaves Has lodged within that nook, And choked the mountain brook.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
The weir of fallen maple leaves, caught by the mountain wind across the stream — the water cannot flow through.
Commentary
According to the Kokin Wakashū's headnote, this poem was "composed while crossing the mountain pass of Shiga," describing a mountain stream the poet saw along the road from Kyoto to Ōtsu. The poem likens the maple leaves piled up in the stream, unable to flow on, to a beautiful weir built by the autumn wind.
About the Poet
Harumichi no Tsuraki was a descendant of the ancient and powerful Mononobe clan. In his youth he studied diligently as a monjōshō (student of literature). He was appointed Governor of Iki, but fell ill and died on the journey to take up his post, his ambitions unfulfilled. He left behind no personal poetry collection and is not widely known as a poet, but Fujiwara no Teika, the compiler of the Hyakunin Isshu, evidently admired this poem enough to allude to it in his own verse — a sign of how highly he regarded it.

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

What was damming the stream
was not branches, but fallen leaves.

A view only autumn could paint —
I stop, and find myself lost in it.
