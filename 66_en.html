Poem 66
もろともに あはわれと思おもへえ 山桜やまざくら
花はなよりほかに 知しる人ひともなし
Author: Senior High Priest Gyōson (1055–1135)
Source: Kin'yō Wakashū, Miscellaneous
English Verse Translation
In lonely solitude I dwell, No human face I see; And so we two must sympathize, Oh mountain cherry tree; I have no friend but thee.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
O mountain cherry — feel moved together with me. There is no one else here who knows me but you.
Commentary
The Kin'yō Wakashū says this was written on unexpectedly seeing cherry blossoms in Ōmine. Gyōson was training as a mountain monk deep in the Ōmine mountains. A lone cherry tree in bloom may have seemed like himself enduring hard training, as if he had met a true friend, a feeling close to Ki no Tsurayuki's poem (Poem 35).
About the Poet
Gyōson was a great-grandson of Retired Emperor Sanjō. He lost his father at 10 and became a monk at Mii-dera at 12, studying esoteric Buddhism. After long mountain training he headed Mii-dera and Tennō-ji, and became Senior High Priest (Daisōjō) at 71.
