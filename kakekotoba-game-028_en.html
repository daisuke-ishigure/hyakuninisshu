Spin & Discover Kakekotoba!
Poem 28: Minamoto no Muneyuki Ason

Try to find the kakekotoba hidden in the poem and tap it.
The word you tap will spin right where it is, revealing its two meanings in turn.

山里は
冬ぞさびしさ
まさりける
人目も草も
かれぬと思へば

Commentary

The word "karenu" is a kakekotoba.
A kakekotoba layers two meanings onto a single word that sounds the same either way.

Here, "karenu" carries both "枯かれぬ" (has withered) and "離かれぬ" (has drifted away, stopped visiting).

By letting a single word, "karenu," receive two very different things at once — "people's visits" and "the grasses" — the poem lets us feel all the more deeply the loneliness of a mountain village in winter, where visitors have stopped coming and the plants have all withered away.

Packing two meanings into just thirty-one syllables is exactly what makes kakekotoba so appealing.

Vocabulary

冬ぞさびしさ

It is winter above all when the loneliness. "Zo" adds emphasis.

まさりける

Grows even deeper. "Keru" expresses exclamation.

人目ひとめ

People coming to visit.

枯かれぬ

The plants have withered.

離かれぬ

People have drifted away and stopped visiting.
