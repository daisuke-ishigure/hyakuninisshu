Spin & Discover Kakekotoba!
Poem 62: Sei Shōnagon

Try to find the kakekotoba hidden in the poem and tap it.
The word you tap will spin right where it is, revealing its two meanings in turn.

夜をこめて
鳥のそらねは
はかるとも
よに逢坂の
関は許さじ

Commentary

The word "Ōsaka" is a kakekotoba.
A kakekotoba layers two meanings onto a single word that sounds the same either way.

Here, "Ōsaka" carries both the place name "逢坂おうさか" (Ōsaka) and "逢あふう" (to meet).

You may fool the Hangu Pass with a rooster's crow, as in the old Chinese legend — but the Ōsaka Pass, never. Layering the meaning "to meet you" onto the place name "Ōsaka," the poet gently but wittily turns down a man's advances.

Packing two meanings into just thirty-one syllables is exactly what makes kakekotoba so appealing.

Vocabulary

夜をこめて

While it is still before dawn.

とりのそらね

Imitating a rooster's crow. A phrase drawn from an old Chinese legend.

はかるとも

Even if you try to deceive me.

よに

Never, absolutely not. Followed by a negative.

逢坂おうさかの関せき

A checkpoint on the border between Yamashiro and Ōmi Provinces.
Its ruins lie in present-day Ōtsu City, Shiga Prefecture.

許ゆるさじ

Will surely never allow. "Ji" expresses negative intention.
