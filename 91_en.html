Poem 91
きりぎりす 鳴なくや霜夜しもよの さむしろに
衣ころもかたしき ひとりかも寝ねむん
Author: Gokyōgoku Regent and former Chancellor of the Realm (1169–1206)
Source: Shin Kokin Wakashū, Autumn
English Verse Translation
I'm sleeping all alone, and hear The crickets round my head; So cold and frosty is the night, That I across the bed My koromo have spread.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
On a cold, frosty night, the cricket cries. Lying alone with one sleeve folded beneath me — how lonely it is.
Commentary
This poem was composed by Kujō Yoshitsune, expressing the loneliness of sleeping alone on an autumn night after the loss of his wife. In those days, when a man and woman slept together, each would spread out the other's robe as a kind of pillow — so the phrase "spreading only my own robe" was a conventional way of expressing sleeping alone.
About the Poet
The Gokyōgoku Regent and former Chancellor of the Realm, Kujō Yoshitsune, was a grandson of the Lay Novice of Hosshō-ji Temple, former Kampaku and Chancellor of the Realm, Fujiwara no Tadamichi (Poem 76), and the second son of Kujō Kanezane. He inherited headship of the Kujō family after his elder brother Yoshimichi died young at twenty-two. He became Regent to Emperor Tsuchimikado and reached the height of his career as Senior First Rank Regent and Chancellor of the Realm — but just two years later, at only thirty-eight, he died before his own father Kanezane. According to one theory, he was assassinated in his sleep, stabbed through the ceiling with a spear.

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

Only the cricket's cry
announces this lonely night.

Lying here with just one thin robe spread beneath me,
I feel the full weight of a love now lost.
