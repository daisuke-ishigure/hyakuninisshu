Poem 91
きりぎりす 鳴なくや霜夜しもよの さむしろに
衣ころもかたしき ひとりかも寝ねむん
Author: Gokyōgoku Regent and former Chancellor of the Realm (1169–1206)
Source: Shin Kokin Wakashū, Autumn
English Verse Translation
I'm sleeping all alone, and hear The crickets round my head; So cold and frosty is the night, That I across the bed My koromo have spread.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
On a cold, frosty night, the cricket cries. Lying alone with one sleeve folded beneath me — how lonely it is.
Commentary
Kujō Yoshitsune wrote this about sleeping alone on an autumn night after his wife died. Lovers then used each other's robes as pillows, so "spreading only my own robe" meant sleeping alone.
About the Poet
Kujō Yoshitsune was the second son of Kujō Kanezane and grandson of Fujiwara no Tadamichi. He became head of the family after his elder brother died young. He rose to regent and Chancellor, but died two years later at 38, before his father. One rumor says he was killed in his sleep by a spear thrust through the ceiling.
