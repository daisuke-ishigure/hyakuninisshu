Poem 44
逢あふうことの 絶たえてしなくは なかなかに
人ひとをも身みをも 恨うらみざらまし
Author: Middle Counselor Asatada (910–967)
Source: Shūi Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
To fall in love with womankind Is my unlucky fate; If only it were otherwise, I might appreciate Some men, whom now I hate.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
If there were no meetings at all, I would not have to suffer. It is because we meet that parting brings such pain.
Commentary
Written for the Tentoku palace poetry contest. It can be read as "love not yet met" or as "love met, then lost." In the second reading, after a happy meeting she turned cold, and he regrets ever meeting her, yet still cannot forget their time together.
About the Poet
Fujiwara no Asatada was the fifth son of Fujiwara no Sadakata, the Minister of the Right of Sanjō and rose to Middle Counselor. Besides poetry, he was a master of the shō, a wind instrument. His granddaughter Tomoko became the wife of Fujiwara no Michinaga.

Fujiwara no Asatada was the fifth son of the Minister of the Right of Sanjō (Fujiwara no Sadakata). His granddaughter Tomoko became the principal wife of Fujiwara no Michinaga. He was one of the Thirty-Six Immortal Poets and a master of the shō.

Asatada's Hyakunin Isshu poem was written at the Palace Poetry Contest of the Tentoku Era, the same contest where the poems of Taira no Kanemori (Poem 40) and Mibu no Tadami (Poem 41) were written.

Appendix
The Shō

A wind instrument used in gagaku (court music). Seventeen thin bamboo pipes are set in a circle, and the player holds it upright to play.

The shō in this photo was made in the 19th century, so it may be a little different from those of the Heian period.
