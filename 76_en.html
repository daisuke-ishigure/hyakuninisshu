Poem 76
わたの原はら こぎいでてみれば 久方ひさかたの
雲くも居いにまがごふう 沖おきつ白波しらなみ
Author: Lay Novice of Hosshō-ji Temple, former Kampaku and Chancellor of the Realm (1097–1164)
Source: Shika Wakashū, Miscellaneous
English Verse Translation
When rowing on the open sea, The waves, all capped with white, Roll onward, like the fleecy clouds With their resistless might; Truly a wondrous sight!
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
Rowing out onto the open sea and looking around — the white waves far off on the water seem to blur into the clouds.
Commentary
This poem was composed for a poetry contest held at the imperial palace during the reign of Retired Emperor Sutoku (Poem 77), on the set topic of "a distant view over the sea."
About the Poet
The Lay Novice of Hosshō-ji Temple, former Kampaku and Chancellor of the Realm, was better known as Fujiwara no Tadamichi, the eldest son of the Regent Fujiwara no Tadazane. Having no son of his own to inherit the leadership of the Fujiwara regental line, Tadamichi first adopted his younger half-brother Yorinaga as heir. When a son of his own was later born, however, he broke off the adoption in order to pass the family headship to his own child — a decision that sparked a bitter feud with Yorinaga, one that eventually erupted into the Hōgen Rebellion. In that conflict, Tadamichi sided with Retired Emperor Go-Shirakawa and emerged victorious, rising to the very pinnacle of the Fujiwara clan. He was also a gifted calligrapher, and is remembered as the founder of the Hosshō-ji school of calligraphy.

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

Before the boundless open sea,
even my heart feels set free, far and wide.

The beauty woven by white waves and white clouds
was enough to make me forget words.
