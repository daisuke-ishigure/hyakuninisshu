Poem 76
わたの原はら こぎいでてみれば 久方ひさかたの
雲くも居いにまがごふう 沖おきつ白波しらなみ
Author: Lay Novice of Hosshō-ji Temple, former Kampaku and Chancellor of the Realm (1097–1164)
Source: Shika Wakashū, Miscellaneous
English Verse Translation
When rowing on the open sea, The waves, all capped with white, Roll onward, like the fleecy clouds With their resistless might; Truly a wondrous sight!
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
Rowing out onto the open sea and looking around — the white waves far off on the water seem to blur into the clouds.
Commentary
Written for a palace poetry contest held while Sutoku (Poem 77) was emperor, on the theme "a distant view over the sea."
About the Poet
Fujiwara no Tadamichi was the eldest son of regent Fujiwara no Tadazane. With no son, he adopted his half-brother Yorinaga, but when a son was born he cancelled the adoption. The feud this caused led to the Hōgen Rebellion, where Tadamichi sided with Go-Shirakawa, won, and became head of the Fujiwara. He also founded the Hosshō-ji school of calligraphy.
