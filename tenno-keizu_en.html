Imperial Family Tree

The Hyakunin Isshu begins with a father and daughter, Emperor Tenji (Poem 1) and Empress Jitō (Poem 2), and ends with a father and son, Retired Emperor Go-Toba (Poem 99) and Retired Emperor Juntoku (Poem 100).
Besides emperors, it includes many members of the imperial family, such as Prince Motoyoshi and Princess Shikishi, and many poets descended from emperors, such as Ariwara no Narihira, Minamoto no Tōru, and Sei Shōnagon.
Many members of the imperial family received a clan name (uji) such as Minamoto, Taira, or Ariwara and became subjects of the emperor. This is called shinseki kōka (leaving the imperial family to become a subject). By following these poets' family lines, you can see which emperor they go back to.

Trivia

Shinseki Kōka: Members of the Imperial Family Who Became Subjects

In the Heian period, emperors usually had several consorts (empresses, nyōgo, and kōi), so many princes and princesses were born. As the imperial family grew larger with each generation, the cost of supporting it also grew.
So some members of the imperial family were given a clan name (uji) such as "Minamoto," "Taira," or "Ariwara," left their status as members of the imperial family, and became subjects. This is called shinseki kōka. Some of them went on to serve the court as officials. Today, when someone leaves the imperial family it is called kōseki ridatsu, which is a different system and idea from shinseki kōka.

  • Genji (the Minamoto):
    This began when Emperor Saga gave his sons and daughters the clan name "Minamoto."
    They are told apart by the name of the emperor they came from: the Saga Genji, Seiwa Genji, Uda Genji, Murakami Genji, and so on.
  • Heishi (the Taira):
    This began when Prince Takamune, a grandson of Emperor Kanmu, received the clan name "Taira."
    Taira no Kiyomori was also of the Kanmu Heishi, but not of Prince Takamune's line: he came from the line of Prince Takamochi, who was also a grandson of Prince Kazurawara.
  • Ariwara clan:
    Yukihira, Narihira, and other sons of Prince Abo, a son of Emperor Heizei, received the clan name "Ariwara."

"Tennō" (Emperor) and "In" (Retired Emperor)

Among the poet names in the Hyakunin Isshu, Emperor Tenji, Empress Jitō, and Emperor Kōkō are called tennō (emperor), but Yōzei, Sanjō, Sutoku, Go-Toba, and Juntoku are called in. In English, this site calls them "Retired Emperor."
In was a name for the residence of an emperor who had given up the throne (a retired emperor), and for that person. In the Heian period, it was common to call a retired emperor "○○-in."
