Poem 85
夜よもすがら 物もの思おもふうころは 明あけやらで
閨ねやのひまさへえ つれなかりけり
Author: Priest Shun'e (1113–death year unknown)
Source: Senzai Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
All through the never-ending night I lie awake and think; In vain I look to try and see The daybreak's feeble blink Peep through the shutter's chink.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
All through the night, lost in thought, the dawn will not come. Even the crack of light through the bedroom door seems cold and distant.
Commentary
The headnote in the Senzai Wakashū reads, "composed as a poem on love" — this was a poem on an assigned topic. Shun'e wrote it from the perspective of a woman, worn out from waiting through an endless darkness, tormented by thoughts of an indifferent lover.
About the Poet
Priest Shun'e lost his father at seventeen and became a monk at Tōdai-ji Temple, famous for its Great Buddha. He later settled in Shirakawa, in Kyoto, where he invited many poets to a monastic retreat he called the Karin-en ("Poetry Grove"), hosting numerous poetry gatherings — many of the Hyakunin Isshu's own poets took part. Among his students was Kamo no Chōmei, author of An Account of My Hut (Hōjōki), who recorded many of Shun'e's teachings in his own critical work, the Mumyōshō.

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

The night refuses to end.
Only my heart, waiting for you, quietly keeps counting the hours.

Not even the light that announces dawn reaches me —
my longing alone is left behind in the dark.
