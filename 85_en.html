Poem 85
夜よもすがら 物もの思おもふうころは 明あけやらで
閨ねやのひまさへえ つれなかりけり
Author: Priest Shun'e (1113–c. 1191)
Source: Senzai Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
All through the never-ending night I lie awake and think; In vain I look to try and see The daybreak's feeble blink Peep through the shutter's chink.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
All through the night, lost in thought, the dawn will not come. Even the crack of light through the bedroom door seems cold and distant.
Commentary
Written on the theme "love." Shun'e speaks as a woman waiting, worn out, through an endless dark night, troubled by thoughts of her cold lover.
About the Poet
Shun'e lost his father, Minamoto no Toshiyori, at 17 and became a monk at Tōdai-ji. He later moved to Shirakawa in Kyoto, called his lodging Karin'en (Poetry Grove), and held many gatherings there, joined by many Hyakunin Isshu poets. His student Kamo no Chōmei, author of the Hōjōki, wrote down many of his teachings.

Priest Shun'e was a descendant of the Uda Genji, the Minamoto line founded by Emperor Uda.

His grandfather was Minamoto no Tsunenobu, and his father was Minamoto no Toshiyori.
Three generations were chosen for the Hyakunin Isshu.

Shun'e lost his father at 17 and became a monk at Tōdai-ji, a temple famous for its Great Buddha.
