Poem 78
淡路島あわじしま かよふう千鳥ちどりの 鳴なく声こえに
幾夜いくよ寝覚ねざめぬ 須磨すまの関守せきもり
Author: Minamoto no Kanemasa (dates unknown)
Source: Kin'yō Wakashū, Winter
English Verse Translation
Between Awaji and the shore The birds scream in their flight Full oft they've made the Suma Guard Toss through a sleepless night, Until the morning light.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
The cry of the plovers flying between Awaji Island — how many nights have I lain awake listening to them at the Suma Barrier?
Commentary
The Kin'yō Wakashū says this was written on the theme "plovers on the barrier road." Suma is where Hikaru Genji withdrew into exile in The Tale of Genji. There Genji hears plovers and writes of sleeping alone, and this poem is thought to draw on that scene.
About the Poet
Minamoto no Kanemasa was a descendant of Emperor Uda but stayed at a low rank. He made his name as a poet and wrote for the Eikyū Hyakushu, a set of a hundred poems ordered by Emperor Toba.

Minamoto no Kanemasa was a descendant of the Uda Genji, the Minamoto line founded by Emperor Uda.

His father, Minamoto no 俊輔 (reading of the name unknown), served as Assistant Governor of Mino.
Kanemasa's final post and rank were Junior Secretary of the Empress's Household and Junior Fifth Rank, Lower Grade.

Appendix
The Cry of the Plover
This recording was not made at Suma. It is the cry of a shore bird.
Hikaru Genji's Poem
In this poem, Hikaru Genji hears the plovers crying at Suma and writes of the loneliness of sleeping alone.

In Japanese

友千鳥ともちどり 諸声もろこえになく 暁あかつきは
ひとり寝覚ねざめの 床とこも頼たのもし tomochidori morokoeninaku akatsukiha
hitorinezameno tokomotanomoshi The Tale of Genji, Hikaru Genji

In English

At dawn, when the flock of plovers cries together with me,
even the bed where I wake up alone feels less lonely.

"Naku" means both "(birds) cry" and "(people) weep."

Utagawa Kuniyoshi's Hyakunin Isshu no Uchi

An ukiyo-e print on the Hyakunin Isshu by Utagawa Kuniyoshi, an ukiyo-e artist of the Edo period. Matching the world of the poem, it shows plovers flying over the seashore.
