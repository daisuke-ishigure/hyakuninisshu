Spin & Discover Kakekotoba!
Poem 14: Minister of the Left of Kawara

Try to find the kakekotoba hidden in the poem and tap it.
The word you tap will spin right where it is, revealing its two meanings in turn.

陸奥の
しのぶもぢずり
誰ゆゑに
乱れそめにし
我ならなくに

Commentary

The word "shinobu" is a kakekotoba.
A kakekotoba layers two meanings onto a single word that sounds the same either way.

Here, "shinobu" carries both the place name "信夫しのぶ" (Shinobu) and "忍しのぶ" (to endure in secret).

Like the tangled dye pattern of "shinobu-mojizuri" cloth, woven in the Shinobu region, my heart has begun to grow tangled because of you — even though I had been enduring it all in secret. This aching love is expressed by layering the feeling onto a place name.

Packing two meanings into just thirty-one syllables is exactly what makes kakekotoba so appealing.

Vocabulary

陸奥むつ

A broad region centered on the Pacific side of what is now the Tōhoku area — Fukushima, Miyagi, Iwate, Aomori, and part of Akita (the northeast).

信夫しのぶ

A place in the Shinobu district of Mutsu (around present-day Fukushima City), known for a dyeing technique called "shinobu-mojizuri."

忍しのぶ

To hide one's feelings from others, to endure.

もぢずり

A dyeing technique from the Shinobu region that used plants to print a tangled pattern onto cloth.

誰ゆゑに

Because of whom.

乱れそめにし

Has begun to grow tangled.

我ならなくに

Even though it is not me. "Nakuni" expresses a negated exclamation.
