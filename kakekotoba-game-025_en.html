Spin & Discover Kakekotoba!
Poem 25: Minister of the Right of Sanjō

Try to find the kakekotoba hidden in the poem and tap it.
The word you tap will spin right where it is, revealing its two meanings in turn.

名にし負はば
逢坂山の
さねかづら
人にしられで
くるよしもがな

Commentary

This poem has three kakekotoba hidden within it.
A kakekotoba layers two meanings onto a single word that sounds the same either way.

The first is "Ōsaka." It carries both the place name "逢坂おうさか" (Ōsaka) and "逢あう" (to meet).

The second is "sanekazura." It carries both the name of a vine plant, "sanekazura," and "さ寝さね" (to sleep together).

The third is "kuru." It carries both "来くる" (to come) and "繰くる" (to reel in).

Like the sanekazura vine growing on Mount Ōsaka, if only there were some way for you to reel me in and come to me without anyone knowing — this aching love is expressed by layering all three kakekotoba together.

Packing so many meanings into just thirty-one syllables is exactly what makes kakekotoba so appealing.

Vocabulary

逢坂山あふさかやま

A 325-meter mountain on the border of Kyoto City and Ōtsu City, Shiga Prefecture.
A checkpoint once stood here, at this important crossroads.

逢あう

To meet someone.

さ寝さね

To sleep together.

繰くる

To reel in a vine, hand over hand.

名にし負はば

If it truly lives up to its name.

人にしられで

Without anyone knowing.

よしもがな

If only there were a way. "Mogana" expresses a wish.
