Poem 72
音おとに聞きく 高師たかしの浜はまの あだ波なみは
かけじや袖そでの ぬれもこそすれ
Author: Kii of Princess Yūshi's Household (dates unknown)
Source: Kin'yō Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
The sound of ripples on the shore, E'er fails at Takashi; My sleeves all worn and wet with tears, Should surely prove to thee, too, will constant be.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
I have heard how fickle the waves at Takashi Beach are. I will not let them wet my sleeves — I will keep my heart guarded.
Commentary
Written for the Horikawa love-letter contest, where men sent love poems and women replied. Fujiwara no Toshitada, 29, wrote that he secretly loved her and wished to tell her at night, as waves come in on the shore wind. Kii, over 70, replied smartly that she would not trust such fickle waves.
About the Poet
Kii served Princess Yūshi, a daughter of Emperor Go-Suzaku, and was active as a poet for many years. Some records say she was the sister or wife of Fujiwara no Shigetsune, governor of Kii, and her name probably comes from that post.

Appendix
Fujiwara no Toshitada's Poem
Kii's Hyakunin Isshu poem was written in reply to the following poem by Fujiwara no Toshitada.

In Japanese

人ひと知しれぬ 思おもひあり その浦風うらかぜに
波なみのよるこそ 言いはわまほしけれ Kin'yō Wakashū, Fujiwara no Toshitada

In English

I love you in secret. As waves come in (yoru) on the shore wind of Ariso, I long to tell you my feelings at night (yoru).

"Omoi ariso no" contains "Arisoumi," a famous poetic place (utamakura) in Takaoka City, Toyama Prefecture.

Hamadera Park, Where Part of the Takashi Beach Remains

The area from Hamadera in Sakai City to Takashi in Takaishi City, Osaka Prefecture. It was once a scenic spot with pine woods along a sandy beach. Today it is a coastal industrial area, and the beach has been replaced by concrete seawalls.

But Hamadera Park was built here, and part of the old pine woods remains in the park, so you can still imagine how it once was.

Ōkubo Toshimichi's Poem That Saved
the Pines of Takashi Beach
In the Meiji period, new rice fields were being developed at Takashi Beach, and its pine woods were in danger of being cut down. But when Ōkubo Toshimichi visited and saw the pines being cut, he was saddened and wrote the next poem. Because of this poem, the cutting was stopped, and later Hamadera Park was created.

In Japanese

音おとに聞きく 高師たかしの浜はまの 浜松はままつも
世よのあだ波なみは のがれざりけり Monument to Treasured Pines (Sekishōhi), Hamadera Park, Ōkubo Toshimichi

In English

Even the beach pines of famous Takashi Beach
could not escape the fickle waves of people's changing hearts.
Ukiyo-e by Utagawa Hiroshige

"Takashi Beach" by Utagawa Hiroshige, an ukiyo-e artist of the Edo period. It shows that this was once a scenic spot of white sand and green pines.
