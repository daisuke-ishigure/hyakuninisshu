Poem 94
み吉野よしのの 山やまの秋風あきかぜ さ夜よふけて
ふるさと寒さむく 衣ころもうつなり
Author: Councillor Masatsune (1170–1221)
Source: Shin Kokin Wakashū, Autumn
English Verse Translation
Around Mount Miyoshino's crest The autumn winds blow drear; The villagers are beating cloth, Their merry din I hear, This night so cold and clear.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
On an autumn night, the mountain wind of Yoshino blows cold. In the old village, I can hear the sound of someone beating cloth.
Commentary
Written on the theme of beating cloth (tōi): striking damp cloth with a wooden mallet to set its folds and give it shine. The poem borrows from Sakanoue no Korenori's poem about snow on the Yoshino mountains and the growing cold in the old capital. The image of beating cloth may also come from a Chinese poem by Li Bai.
About the Poet
Fujiwara no Masatsune was a master of kemari (court football) and founded the Asukai school of the game. He studied waka under Fujiwara no Shunzei and was chosen as a compiler of the Shin Kokin Wakashū.
