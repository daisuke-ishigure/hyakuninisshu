Poem 25
名なにし負おはば 逢坂山おうさかやまの さねかづずら
人ひとにしられで くるよしもがな
Author: Minister of the Right of Sanjō (873–932)
Source: Gosen Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
Hear thou art as modest as The little creeping spray Upon Mount Ōsaka, which hides Beneath the grass; then, pray, Wander with me to-day.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
If the name of Ōsaka carries its meaning — then let me secretly follow the vine I draw toward me, and come to you unseen.
Note: "Ōsaka" is both a place name and a pun on the verb *au* ("to meet"). The vine (*kazura*) reflects the wordplay of the original poem: to pull or draw the vine (*kuru*) and to come (*kuru*) to one's beloved unseen.
Commentary
As Minister of the Right, the poet held an extremely high rank, which likely made it difficult for him to meet his lover freely. This poem may express his wish to see her in secret, away from prying eyes. People of the time had a custom of sending along the very object named in a poem as a gift — perhaps he sent this poem together with an actual sanekazura vine.
About the Poet
The Minister of the Right of Sanjō, Fujiwara no Sadakata, was the father of Middle Counselor Asatada. He was called "the Sanjō Minister of the Right" because his residence was in the Sanjō district and he held the office of Minister of the Right. As an imperial in-law, he rose to that high rank, and he recognized the talents of Ki no Tsurayuki and Ōshikōchi no Mitsune, supporting their careers as poets.

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

However much I long for you,
I'm not free to see you.

If only I could draw you close
the way one gathers a trailing vine…
