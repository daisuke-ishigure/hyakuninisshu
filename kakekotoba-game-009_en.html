Spin & Discover Kakekotoba!
Poem 9: Ono no Komachi

Try to find the kakekotoba hidden in the poem and tap it.
The word you tap will spin right where it is, revealing its two meanings in turn.

花の色は
うつりにけりな
いたづらに
わが身世にふる
ながめせしまに

Commentary

The word "nagame" is a kakekotoba.
A kakekotoba layers two meanings onto a single word that sounds the same either way.

Here, "nagame" carries both "眺めながめ" (gazing absently) and "長雨ながめ" (long spring rains).

The poet's grief over the cherry blossoms fading away in vain overlaps with the image of herself gazing blankly at the steady spring rain, lost in thought — the passing of the season and the passing of her own youth are compressed into a single word.

Packing two scenes into just thirty-one syllables is exactly what makes kakekotoba so appealing.

Vocabulary

花

The color of cherry blossoms — used as a symbol of fleeting beauty.

うつる

To fade, to lose color.

いたづらに

In vain, to no purpose.

降ふる

Rain falls.

経ふる

Time passes.

長雨ながめ

Rain that falls steadily for a long stretch of time.

眺ながめ

Gazing blankly while lost in thought.
