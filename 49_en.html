みかき

Poem 49
御垣守みかきもり 衛士えじのたく火ひの 夜よるはもえ
昼ひるは消きえつつ 物ものをこそ思おもへえ
Author: Ōnakatomi no Yoshinobu Ason (921–991)
Source: Shika Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
My constancy to her I love I never will forsake; As surely as the Palace Guards Each night their watch-fire make And guard it till daybreak.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
Like the fire tended by the palace guards, I burn at night and fade in the day — lost in endless longing.
Commentary
Mikakimori refers to the guards who watched over the gates of the imperial palace, and in this poem functions as a pillow word leading into eji (the palace watchmen). The poem likens the watch-fires burning before the gate to the flame of the poet's love, expressing his aching inability to meet his beloved during the daylight hours when the fires are put out.
About the Poet
Ōnakatomi no Yoshinobu Ason belonged to the Nakatomi clan, sharing the same ancestry as Nakatomi no Kamatari, a family originally charged with overseeing Shinto rites. He himself served as chief priest of the Ise Grand Shrine, fulfilling duties within the Department of Divinities. Many poets came from his family line — Ise no Taifu (Poem 61) was his granddaughter. He is also known as one of the "Five Men of the Pear Jar Room," who compiled the Gosen Wakashū on the order of Emperor Murakami.

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

At night, the feeling deep in my chest
burns red like a watch-fire.

By day, unable to see you,
I pass the hours as if I might disappear.
