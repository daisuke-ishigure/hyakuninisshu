Poem 11
わたの原はら 八十島やそしまかけて 漕こぎ出いでぬと
人ひとにはつげよ あまのつり舟ぶね
Author: Councillor Takamura (802–852)
Source: Kokin Wakashū, Travel
English Verse Translation
Oh! Fishers in your little boats, Quick! tell my men, I pray, They'll find me at Yasoshima, I'm being rowed away Far off across the bay.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
I have set out rowing across the vast sea, heading for the many islands far away. Tell the people back home.
Commentary
The Kokin Wakashū's headnote reads, "Sent to someone in the capital as he set out by boat, having been exiled to Oki Province." Takamura composed this poem for the family he left behind in the capital, as he was being banished to the island of Oki.
About the Poet
Councillor Takamura's real name was Ono no Takamura, and he was the grandfather of Ono no Komachi. While traveling to Tang China as deputy envoy, he was ordered by a superior to swap ships with a leaking vessel, and he refused. He further angered Retired Emperor Saga by writing a Chinese poem, "Saidōyō," criticizing the incompetence of the embassy, and was banished to the remote island of Oki, where he endured a harsh existence. Two years later he was pardoned, returned to the capital, and was restored to office.
Councillor Takamura's Chart of the Heart

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

I row out toward the edge of the endless sea.
Tell my family waiting in the capital how I looked.

Alone on this vast ocean, with nothing to hold onto,
I picture their faces in the waves.
