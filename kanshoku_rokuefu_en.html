Security and Police Offices

In the Heian capital, separate offices were responsible for guarding the palace, inspecting officials, and maintaining public order in the city. The Rokuefu guarded the palace, the Danjōdai oversaw officials, and the Kebiishi handled police and judicial functions in the capital.

The Six Guards (Rokuefu, 六衛府)

The Rokuefu was the collective name for the six palace guard agencies — each of the three guard offices (Konoefu, Emonfu, and Hyōefu) was divided into a Left and Right branch. By the early Heian period, this six-agency system of palace security was firmly established. Their duties included escorting the emperor, guarding palace gates, and patrolling the palace grounds.

The palace was organized as two nested enclosures: the Dairi (the emperor's private residential compound) sat at the center, surrounded by the larger Daidairi (the greater palace complex containing government offices and ceremonial halls). Konoefu (近衛府) — Imperial Bodyguard

Established in the Nara period, the Konoefu was a ryōgenokan (an office created outside the ritsuryō legal codes). It had a Left division (Sakonoefu) and a Right division (Ukonoefu). The guards patrolled inside the Dairi and escorted the emperor during imperial processions.

Emonfu (衛門府) — Palace Gate Guard

The Emonfu patrolled the Daidairi and escorted the emperor during imperial processions. It had a Left division (Saemonfu) and a Right division (Uemonfu).

Hyōefu (兵衛府) — Dairi Perimeter Guard

The Hyōefu guarded the perimeter around the Dairi and escorted the emperor and crown prince during processions. It had a Left division (Sahyōefu) and a Right division (Uhyōefu).

Danjōdai (弾正台) — Inspector General's Office

The Danjōdai was responsible for investigating misconduct among officials and enforcing public morals and order within the capital. After the Kebiishi was established, the Danjōdai's role was absorbed by that office, and it became largely ceremonial.

Kebiishi (検非違使) — Capital Police

The Kebiishi was a ryōgenokan (an office created outside the ritsuryō legal codes), established during the reign of Emperor Saga to maintain public order in the capital. Over time it absorbed functions previously handled by the Gyōbushō (Ministry of Justice), the Danjōdai, and the Kyōshiki (Capital Office), and also took on judicial responsibilities. Officials from the Emonfu were frequently appointed to the Kebiishi.

The Kebiishi's field operatives included a group known as the hōmen (放免). Composed mainly of former criminals, their familiarity with the underworld made them well suited for tracking down suspects, making arrests, and conducting interrogations.
