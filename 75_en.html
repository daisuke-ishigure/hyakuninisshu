Poem 75
契ちぎりおきし させもが露つゆを いのちにて
あはわれ今年ことしの 秋あきもいぬめり
Author: Fujiwara no Mototoshi (1060–1142)
Source: Senzai Wakashū, Miscellaneous
English Verse Translation
It is a promise unfulfilled, For which I humbly sue The dainty little mugwort plant Relies upon the dew, And I rely—on you.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
You promised me, and I staked my life on it like the morning dew on the wormwood. But another autumn is passing, alas.
Commentary
According to the headnote in the Senzai Wakashū, Mototoshi asked Fujiwara no Tadamichi (Poem 76) to help his son Kōkaku, a monk at Kōfuku-ji Temple, be appointed lecturer at the Yuima-e assembly. Tadamichi replied by quoting a line from an old poem — "On the moor of Shimeji" — seeming to promise his help, but in the end Kōkaku was not chosen for the post. Deeply disappointed, Mototoshi composed this poem.
About the Poet
Mototoshi was a great-grandson of Fujiwara no Michinaga, though his own court rank remained modest, rising no higher than Junior Fifth Rank, Lower. A champion of traditional poetic style, he was often at odds with Minamoto no Toshiyori (Poem 74), an advocate of more innovative forms, though the two men together stood as the twin pillars of poetry during the era of cloistered rule. His students included Fujiwara no Shunzei (Poem 83).

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

Trusting those words,
I waited through the turning of the seasons.

But autumn quietly passed,
leaving only a heart full of sighs.
