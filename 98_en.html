Poem 98
風かぜそよぐ ならの小川おがわの 夕ゆう暮ぐれは
みそぎぞ夏なつの しるしなりける
Author: Junior Second Rank Ietaka (1158–1237)
Source: Shin Chokusen Wakashū, Summer
English Verse Translation
The twilight dim, the gentle breeze By Nara's little stream, The splash of worshippers who wash Before the shrine, all seem A perfect summer's dream.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
The breeze rustles through the leaves of the sacred oak trees by the river at dusk. The purification rite is the only sign that summer is here.
Commentary
Written for a folding screen in 1229, when Kujō Shunshi became a consort of Emperor Go-Horikawa. It went with a painting of the Minazuki purification rite on one of the screens she brought to court.
About the Poet
Fujiwara no Ietaka studied waka under Fujiwara no Toshinari and, with Fujiwara no Sadaie, was a leading poet of his time. Go-Toba chose him as a compiler of the Shin Kokin Wakashū. After Go-Toba lost the Jōkyū War and was exiled to Oki, Ietaka stayed loyal and supported him through letters.

According to one theory, Fujiwara no Ietaka was the son-in-law of Jakuren, Fujiwara no Toshinari's adopted son. Ietaka studied waka under Toshinari and polished his skill until he was ranked alongside Fujiwara no Sadaie, the compiler of the Hyakunin Isshu, as a good rival. Both were chosen as compilers of the Shin Kokin Wakashū.

Ietaka served Retired Emperor Go-Toba. Even after Go-Toba started the Jōkyū War, lost, and was exiled to Oki, Ietaka stayed loyal and kept in touch with him, for example by receiving topics from him and sending him poems.

Ietaka's daughter, Tsuchimikado-in no Kosaishō, first served Retired Emperor Tsuchimikado, and after the Jōkyū War she served Minamoto no Ariko (Shōmeimon-in), Emperor Tsuchimikado's mother.

A Poem About Sue no Matsuyama
Fujiwara no Ietaka wrote about "Sue no Matsuyama," the place in Kiyohara no Motosuke's poem, like this.
He seems to have imagined a dreamlike scene in which trailing clouds at dawn hang over Sue no Matsuyama as if waves were coming over it.

In Japanese

霞かすみ立たつ 末すえの松山まつやま ほのぼのと
波なみに離はなるる 横雲よこぐもの空そら kasumitatsu suenomatsuyama honobonoto
naminihanaruru yokogumonosora Shin Kokin Wakashū, Fujiwara no Ietaka

In English

Haze rises over Sue no Matsuyama, which the waves are said never to cross. In the sky as dawn slowly breaks, trailing clouds part faintly from the waves, as if about to pass over Sue no Matsuyama.
A Poem About Lake Biwa
On the shore of Lake Biwa, in a winter so harsh that the water freezes and the waves draw back toward the open lake.
The poem describes the dawn moon (ariake no tsuki) in the sky on such a bitterly cold morning.

In Japanese

志賀しがの浦うらや 遠とおざかりゆく 波間なみまより
凍こおりていづる 有明ありあけの月つき shiganouraya toozakariyuku namimayori
kooriteizuru ariakenotsuki Shin Kokin Wakashū, Fujiwara no Ietaka

In English

Shiga Bay. From between the waves drawing farther away, the dawn moon appears, frozen in the icy daybreak.
A Poem About Tachibana Flowers
In the Kokin Wakashū there is a famous anonymous poem: "五月さつき待まつ 花橘はなたちばなの 香かをかげば 昔むかしの人ひとの 袖そでの香かぞする" (When I smell the tachibana flowers that wait for the fifth month, I smell the sleeves of someone I once loved). Since then, the scent of tachibana (a kind of citrus) flowers has been thought of as something that brings back the past. Building on this tradition, Ietaka asks a playful question in the next poem.

In Japanese

今年ことしより 花はなさきそむる 橘たちばなの
いかで昔むかしの 香かに匂におふらむ kotoshiyori hanasakisomuru tachibanano
ikademukashino kaniniofuramu Shin Kokin Wakashū, Fujiwara no Ietaka

In English

This tachibana tree only began to bloom this year,
so how can it give off the scent of the past?
A Poem from Ietaka's Last Years
In his last years, Fujiwara no Ietaka became a monk after an illness, moved near Shitennō-ji in what is now Osaka City, and built a hermitage he named Sekiyō-an (the Evening Sun Hermitage). From there he prayed to the sun setting into the western sea, wishing to journey to the Pure Land in the west. The next poem was written at that time. Today the sunset can no longer be seen from this spot, but the area keeps the name "Yūhigaoka-chō" (Evening Sun Hill) in memory of Sekiyō-an.

In Japanese

契ちぎりあれば 難波なにわの里さとに やどりきて
波なみの入日いりびを 拝おがみけるかな chigiriareba naniwanosatoni yadorikite
naminoiribio ogamikerukana Kokon Chomonjū, Fujiwara no Ietaka

In English

Because of a bond from a past life, I came to live in the village of Naniwa, and so I pray to the sun setting among the waves.
Karyū-zuka (Ietaka's Grave)

In Yūhigaoka-chō, Tennōji Ward, Osaka City, is the grave of Fujiwara no Ietaka, known affectionately as Karyū-zuka ("Karyū" is the Chinese-style reading of Ietaka). It is said to have been built on the site of Sekiyō-an, where Ietaka spent his last years.
