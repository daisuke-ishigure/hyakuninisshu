Poem 47
八重葎やえむぐら しげれる宿やどの さびしきに
人ひとこそ見みえね 秋あきは来きにけり
Author: Priest Egyō (dates unknown)
Source: Shūi Wakashū, Autumn
English Verse Translation
My little temple stands alone, No other hut is near; No one will pass to stop and praise Its vine-grown roof, I fear, Now that the autumn's here.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
Weeds grow thick around the deserted house. No one comes to visit. And yet — autumn has arrived.
Commentary
Written at a gathering at Kawara-in on the theme "autumn comes to a ruined house." Built by the Minister of the Left of Kawara (Poem 14), this splendid mansion fell to ruin after his death. His great-grandson, Priest Anpō, lived there and let weeds grow wild, yet poets were inspired by its faded glory and often met there for poetry.
About the Poet
Priest Egyō is said to have been a lecturer at the provincial temple of Harima. He was a friend of Priest Anpō, who lived at Kawara-in, and often visited the mansion.
