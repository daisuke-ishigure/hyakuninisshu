やす

Poem 59
やすらはわで 寝ねなましものを 小夜さよ更ふけて
かたぶくまでの 月つきを見みしかな
Author: Akazome Emon (c. 956–1041)
Source: Go Shūi Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
Waiting and hoping for thy step, Sleepless in bed I lie, All through the night, until the moon, Leaving her post on high, Slips sideways down the sky.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
I waited and waited without sleep, watching the moon sink slowly toward the horizon in the deepening night. How I regret it.
Commentary
According to the headnote in the Go Shūi Wakashū, this poem arose when Fujiwara no Michitaka was courting one of Akazome Emon's sisters. One night, despite having promised to visit, he failed to appear. On her sister's behalf, Akazome Emon composed this poem, gently conveying her displeasure.
About the Poet
Akazome Emon's name comes from her father's office, Right Gate Guards Officer (Uemon no Jō). She served Rinshi, the principal wife of Fujiwara no Michinaga, and later served Rinshi's daughter Shōshi as well, once Shōshi became consort to Emperor Ichijō. During this time she was on friendly terms with both Murasaki Shikibu and Izumi Shikibu. She is also known as the author of A Tale of Flowering Fortunes (Eiga Monogatari).

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

Waiting for you, thinking you'd come any moment,
the moon quietly sank into the western sky.

Had I known you weren't coming,
I wouldn't have spent such a long night waiting.
