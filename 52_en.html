Poem 52
明けぬれば 暮くるるものとは 知しりながら
なほおうらめしき 朝あさぼらけかな
Author: Fujiwara no Michinobu Ason (972–994)
Source: Go Shūi Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
Although I know the gentle night Will surely follow morn, Yet, when I'm wakened by the sun, Turn over, stretch and yawn— How I detest the dawn!
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
I know that after dawn comes dusk — and yet how I resent this pale morning sky.
Commentary
Even knowing they will meet again when night falls, he still finds the dawn parting painful and hateful. In the Heian period lovers met only at night and had to part in the morning. The poem shows his wish not to leave his love even for a moment.
About the Poet
Fujiwara no Michinobu was raised as the adopted son of his uncle, Fujiwara no Kaneie, the chief adviser. The Ōkagami calls him a fine poet of quiet, gentle character. Much was expected of him, but he died of smallpox at only 23.
