Spin & Discover Kakekotoba!
Poem 77: Retired Emperor Sutoku

Try to find the kakekotoba hidden in the poem and tap it.
The word you tap will spin right where it is, revealing its two meanings in turn.

瀬をはやみ
岩にせかるる
滝川の
われても末に
あはむとぞ思ふ

Commentary

This poem has two kakekotoba hidden within it.
A kakekotoba layers two meanings onto a single word that sounds the same either way.

The first is "warete mo." It carries both "割われても" (even when the water splits apart on the rocks) and "別わかれても" (even when we part).

The second is "awamu." It carries both "合あはむ" (the water will come back together) and "逢あはむ" (I will meet you again).

Because the current runs so swiftly, the rushing river is split apart by a rock — yet in the end its waters flow back together as one. Just so, even if we must part for now, I know that someday we will meet again.

The scene of the river is layered onto the poet's hope of parting from, and someday reuniting with, a beloved.

Packing two meanings into just thirty-one syllables is exactly what makes kakekotoba so appealing.

Vocabulary

瀬せをはやみ

Because the river's current runs swiftly.
"~o …mi" means "because it is ~."

せかるる

Is dammed up, blocked.

滝川たきがわ

A swift-flowing river, a torrent.

とぞ思おもふ

I think so. "Zo" adds emphasis.
