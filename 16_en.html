Poem 16
立たち別わかれ いなばの山やまの 峰みねに生おふうる
まつとし聞きかば 今いま帰かえりこむん
Author: Middle Counselor Yukihira (818–893)
Source: Kokin Wakashū, Parting
English Verse Translation
If breezes on Inaba's peak Sigh through the old pine tree, To whisper in my lonely ears That thou dost pine for me,— Swiftly I'll fly to thee.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
I am leaving for the province of Inaba, where the pines grow on the mountain peak. If I hear that you are waiting for me, I will come back right away.
Commentary
This poem was composed for those who came to see him off as he departed the capital to take up his post as Governor of Inaba. Interestingly, this poem is also known as a charm — posted on a doorway, it is said to bring a lost pet back home. Perhaps it is the deep affection and the strong hope for reunion woven into the poem that carries through.
About the Poet
Middle Counselor Yukihira (Ariwara no Yukihira) was the second son of Prince Abo, himself a son of Emperor Heizei, and an elder half-brother of Ariwara no Narihira. A capable official, he rose through posts including Governor of Harima, Governor of Shinano, Head Chamberlain, and Deputy Governor-General of Dazaifu, eventually reaching the rank of Middle Counselor. At sixty-three he founded the Shōgakuin, a school for the Ariwara clan, and was also a devoted educator.

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

Parting is lonely, but
this farewell won't last forever.

If I hear that you're waiting for me,
I will surely come home.
