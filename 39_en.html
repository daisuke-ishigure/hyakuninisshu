Poem 39
浅茅生あさじうの 小野おのの篠原しのはら しのぶれど
あまりてなどか 人ひとの恋こいしき
Author: Councillor Hitoshi (880–951)
Source: Gosen Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
'Tis easier to hide the reeds Upon the moor that grow, Than try to hide the ardent love That sets my cheeks aglow For somebody I know.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
Though I try to hide it, my longing spills over. Why is it that I cannot stop thinking of that person?
Commentary
This poem was composed with an unrequited love in mind, expressing feelings too strong to keep hidden. "Asajiu no ono no shinohara" (the sparse bamboo grass of the moor) serves as a preface leading into the word "shinobu" (to endure / hide). Some argue that "asajiu" and "shinohara" carry no meaning of their own beyond that wordplay, while others read the desolate landscape as mirroring the painful effort of concealing love.
About the Poet
Councillor Hitoshi was a great-grandson of Emperor Saga, but was removed from the imperial family and took the name Minamoto no Hitoshi. He served in a series of provincial posts — Governor of Mikawa, Governor of Tanba, Deputy Governor of Mino, and Governor of Bizen — before finally rising to Councillor and joining the ranks of the court nobility.

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

Alone, I stand
where the wind sweeps through the bamboo grass.

The more I try to hold it back,
the more this longing fills my heart.
