Poem 48
風かぜをいたみ 岩いわうつ波なみの おのれのみ
くだけて物ものを 思おもふうころかな
Author: Minamoto no Shigeyuki (dates unknown–1000)
Source: Shika Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
The waves that dash against the rocks Are broken by the wind And turned to spray; my loving heart Is broken too, I find, Since thou art so unkind.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
Like the waves crashing against the rocks in the fierce wind, I alone am broken to pieces by this sorrow.
Commentary
The poet compares the one he loves to a rock and himself to the waves breaking against it. However hard he tries, his love never reaches her, and the stormy sea shows this sorrow dramatically.
About the Poet
Minamoto no Shigeyuki was a great-grandson of Emperor Seiwa and wrote poetry while serving in provincial posts. He gave the future Emperor Reizei a set of a hundred poems, said to be the oldest such set surviving. He was a friend of Sone no Yoshitada and went with Fujiwara no Sanekata when Sanekata was posted to Mutsu.
