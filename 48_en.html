Poem 48
風かぜをいたみ 岩いわうつ波なみの おのれのみ
くだけて物ものを 思おもふうころかな
Author: Minamoto no Shigeyuki (dates unknown–1000)
Source: Shika Wakashū, Love
English Verse Translation
The waves that dash against the rocks Are broken by the wind And turned to spray; my loving heart Is broken too, I find, Since thou art so unkind.
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
Like the waves crashing against the rocks in the fierce wind, I alone am broken to pieces by this sorrow.
Commentary
The poet compares the one he loves to a rock and himself to the waves breaking against it. However hard he tries, his love never reaches her, and the stormy sea shows this sorrow dramatically.
About the Poet
Minamoto no Shigeyuki was a great-grandson of Emperor Seiwa and wrote poetry while serving in provincial posts. He gave the future Emperor Reizei a set of a hundred poems, said to be the oldest such set surviving. He was a friend of Sone no Yoshitada and went with Fujiwara no Sanekata when Sanekata was posted to Mutsu.

Minamoto no Shigeyuki was the son of Minamoto no Kanenobu, but he was adopted by his uncle, Councillor Minamoto no Kanetada. He was a great-grandson of Emperor Seiwa, but he never rose high and served in one provincial post after another. His final post and rank were Acting Governor of Chikuzen and Junior Fifth Rank, Lower Grade.

He was friends with Minamoto no Shitagō, Sone no Yoshitada, Taira no Kanemori, and others.

Shigeyuki wrote a set of a hundred poems, the Shigeyuki Hyakushu, and presented it to the future Emperor Reizei when he was crown prince. He was influenced by Sone no Yoshitada, who had written a set of a hundred poems earlier, though privately, and the two were friends.

Late in life, when Fujiwara no Sanekata went to Mutsu Province as its governor, Shigeyuki went with him and died there.

Appendix
A Poem About Sleeves Wet with Tears
The next poem is by Minamoto no Shigeyuki and is in the Go Shūi Wakashū.
Later, Attendant to Empress Inpu wrote a new poem based on it, and that poem was chosen for the Hyakunin Isshu.

In Japanese

松島まつしまや 雄島おじまの磯いそに 漁あさりせし
海人あまの袖そでこそ かくは濡ぬれしか Go Shūi Wakashū, Minamoto no Shigeyuki

In English

Would even the sleeves of the divers fishing on the rocky shore of Ojima at Matsushima be as wet as my sleeves, soaked with tears?
