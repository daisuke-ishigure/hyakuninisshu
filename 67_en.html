Poem 67
春はるの夜よの 夢ゆめばかりなる 手枕たまくらに
かひいなく立たたむん 名なこそをしけれ
Author: Suō no Naishi (c. 1037–c. 1111)
Source: Senzai Wakashū, Miscellaneous
English Verse Translation
If I had made thy proffered arm A pillow for my head For but the moment's time, in which A summer's dream had fled, What would the world have said?
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
In the spring night, resting my head on an arm for just a brief dream — how I grieve that my name might spread for such a fleeting thing.
Commentary
One night at the Nijō palace, as people talked till late, Suō no Naishi said she was sleepy and wanted a pillow. Fujiwara no Tadaie slipped his arm under the blinds and said, "Use this as your pillow." She politely turned him down with this poem on the spot. Tadaie was Fujiwara no Teika's great-grandfather.
About the Poet
Suō no Naishi's name comes from her father, governor of Suō; her real name was Taira no Chūshi. She served four emperors, Go-Reizei, Go-Sanjō, Shirakawa, and Horikawa, and was a famous court poet with 35 poems in imperial anthologies.
