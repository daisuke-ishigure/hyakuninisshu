Poem 67
春はるの夜よの 夢ゆめばかりなる 手枕たまくらに
かひいなく立たたむん 名なこそをしけれ
Author: Suō no Naishi (dates unknown)
Source: Senzai Wakashū, Miscellaneous
English Verse Translation
If I had made thy proffered arm A pillow for my head For but the moment's time, in which A summer's dream had fled, What would the world have said?
— William N. Porter, 1909
Modern Translation
In the spring night, resting my head on an arm for just a brief dream — how I grieve that my name might spread for such a fleeting thing.
Commentary
One night, while people at the Nijō-in Palace were talking through the night, Suō no Naishi remarked that she was growing sleepy and wished she had a pillow. Major Counselor Fujiwara no Tadaie then quietly slipped his arm out from behind the blinds, offering, "Please, use this as your pillow." In response to this elegant gesture, Suō no Naishi composed this poem on the spot, gently declining his offer. Fujiwara no Tadaie, incidentally, was the great-grandfather of Fujiwara no Teika (Poem 97).
About the Poet
Suō no Naishi's court name comes from her father's post as Governor of Suō; her given name was Taira no Chūshi. She served four successive emperors — Go-Reizei, Go-Sanjō, Shirakawa, and Horikawa — and made her name as a court poet. Her talent was highly regarded, and thirty-five of her poems were selected for imperial anthologies from the Go Shūi Wakashū onward.

The Poet's Feelings (A Personal Interpretation)

If it's only a fleeting game,
like a dream on a spring night —

I'd rather laugh it off gently
than let my name be ruined over something so brief.
