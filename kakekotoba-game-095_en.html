Spin & Discover Kakekotoba!
Poem 95: Former Senior High Priest Jien

Try to find the kakekotoba hidden in the poem and tap it.
The word you tap will spin right where it is, revealing its two meanings in turn.

おほけなく
うき世の民に
おほふかな
わがたつ杣に
すみぞめの袖

Commentary

The word "sumizome" is a kakekotoba.
A kakekotoba layers two meanings onto a single word that sounds the same either way.

Here, "sumizome" carries both "墨染すみぞめ" (sumizome, ink-dyed robes) and "住すみ初そめ" (sumizome, just begun to live [on the mountain]).

Beyond my station though it may be, I find myself wishing to shelter the people of this troubled world beneath the sleeves of these ink-dyed robes — the robes of one who has only just begun to live on this mountain.

The poem layers the color of ink-dyed cloth with the poet's own recent arrival at Mount Hiei, expressing a monk's resolve to save the people of the world.

Packing two meanings into just thirty-one syllables is exactly what makes kakekotoba so appealing.

Vocabulary

おほけなく

Presumptuously, beyond what one deserves.

うき世よ

This painful, troubled world.

おほふかな

I find myself wishing to shelter [them].
Expresses a wish to protect and embrace the people.

わがたつ杣そま

The mountain where I live. Refers to Mount Hiei. A phrase drawn from a poem by Saichō.

墨染すみぞめ

Robes dyed the color of ink.
In this poem, refers to a monk's robes.

袖そで

The sleeve of a robe.
