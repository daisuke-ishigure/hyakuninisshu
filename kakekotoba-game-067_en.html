Spin & Discover Kakekotoba!
Poem 67: Suō no Naishi

Try to find the kakekotoba hidden in the poem and tap it.
The word you tap will spin right where it is, revealing its two meanings in turn.

春の夜の
夢ばかりなる
手枕に
かひなく立たむ
名こそをしけれ

Commentary

The word "kaina" is a kakekotoba.
A kakekotoba layers two meanings onto a single word that sounds the same either way.

Here, "kaina" carries both "腕かひな" (arm) and "甲斐かいなく" (in vain, to no avail).

On a spring night, for a time as brief as a dream, if resting my head on your arm should give rise to nothing but empty rumors, that would be a regrettable, wasted thing indeed.

The poet layers "kaina" (arm) together with "kainaku" (in vain), composed in the midst of a witty exchange.

Packing two meanings into just thirty-one syllables is exactly what makes kakekotoba so appealing.

Vocabulary

甲斐かいなく

In vain, to no purpose.

手枕たまくら

Resting one's head on someone's arm as a pillow.

立たたむ

Will likely arise. Here, refers to a rumor spreading.

名こそをしけれ

It is precisely the rumor spreading that would be regrettable.
